What is Mohs Micrographic Surgery?

Mohs surgery is a precise surgical technique used to treat skin cancer. During the procedure, thin layers of cancer-containing skin are progressively removed and examined until only cancer-free tissue remains.

This technique allows for complete removal of the cancer while sparing as much healthy tissue as possible—especially important for cancers on the face, ears, nose, and other cosmetically sensitive areas.

Named after Dr. Frederic Mohs who developed the technique, Mohs surgery has become the gold standard for treating basal cell carcinoma and squamous cell carcinoma, offering the highest cure rates of any treatment method.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Mohs surgery painful?

Mohs surgery is performed under local anesthesia, so you won't feel pain during the procedure. Some patients experience mild discomfort during healing, which can be managed with over-the-counter pain medication.

How long does Mohs surgery take?

The procedure typically takes 2-4 hours, though this varies based on the size and location of the cancer. Plan to spend most of the day at our office, as there is waiting time between each stage while tissue is processed.

What types of skin cancer is Mohs used for?

Mohs surgery is primarily used for basal cell carcinoma and squamous cell carcinoma, especially when located on the face, ears, nose, lips, or other cosmetically sensitive areas. It's also used for recurrent cancers and aggressive tumor types.

Will I have a scar?

All skin cancer removal results in some scarring. However, because Mohs surgery removes the least amount of tissue necessary, scars are typically smaller than with other methods. Dr. Sunseri is skilled in reconstruction techniques to minimize scarring.

Does insurance cover Mohs surgery?

Yes, Mohs surgery is a medically necessary procedure and is covered by most insurance plans, including Medicare. Our staff will verify your coverage and discuss any costs before your procedure.
